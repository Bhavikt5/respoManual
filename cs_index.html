Module 01
Who We Are and What Customer Service Means
Understanding Respo's model and your role as the borrower's primary point of contact

About Respo Financial Capital

Respo Financial Capital Private Limited is an RBI-registered Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC). We provide unsecured personal loans to individuals through the Zype app, operated by Easy Platform Services Private Limited.

Our loans range from 6 to 36 months in tenure and are disbursed entirely digitally — no physical branches, no cash. Every borrower interaction happens through the Zype app, our helpline, or email.

Our Promise to Every Borrower: Respo is committed to providing credit in a fair, transparent, and responsible manner. This commitment is supported by the Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025 and Respo's Fair Practices Code.

Your Role in Customer Service

As a member of the customer service team, you are the borrower's primary point of contact at Respo. You represent Respo in every interaction. Your role is to:

  • Help borrowers understand their loan products, terms, and account status
  • Resolve service requests and complaints fairly, promptly, and with empathy
  • Escalate issues that are beyond your authority to the right person — quickly
  • Ensure every borrower is treated with dignity and respect regardless of their situation

Module 02
Understanding Our Products and Borrowers
What our borrowers have taken, what they are entitled to know, and how to find their account details

Our Loan Product

Respo offers one product: unsecured personal loans through the Zype app. Key features every customer service agent must know:

Feature Detail
Loan type Unsecured personal loan — no collateral required
Tenure 6 months to 36 months
Disbursement 100% digital — directly to borrower's bank account via NEFT/IMPS
Repayment Equated Monthly Instalments (EMIs) via eNACH / UPI AutoPay
Interest rate As disclosed in the Key Fact Statement (KFS) — varies by borrower risk profile
Penal charges Late Payment Penalty — capped at ₹2,500 per loan. NOT capitalised. NOT interest.
Cash acceptance Respo does not accept cash. All transactions via Zype app or bank transfer only.

The Key Fact Statement (KFS) — Your Most Important Document

Mandatory: Under Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, every borrower must receive a Key Fact Statement (KFS) before signing the loan agreement. The KFS is a one-page summary of all key loan terms in plain language. As a customer service agent, you must understand the KFS and be able to explain it to borrowers.

The KFS must contain the information prescribed under the applicable RBI KFS framework, including:

  • Loan amount, tenure, and disbursement details
  • Annual Percentage Rate (APR) — the all-inclusive cost of the loan
  • EMI amount and schedule
  • All fees and charges (processing fee, penal charges)
  • Cooling-off period details
  • Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) contact details
  • Recovery mechanism and grievance redressal details
Warning: If a borrower says they did not receive the KFS or the KFS was not explained before execution of the loan contract, treat it as a high-priority service request. Verify delivery, resend the document where appropriate, log the complaint and escalate. The KFS must be provided before execution of the loan contract and its contents explained to the borrower.

Key Terms Every Agent Must Know

APR Annual Percentage Rate — the total cost of the loan including all fees and charges, expressed as an annual percentage
EMI Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed monthly repayment amount

Module 03
Handling Enquiries and Service Requests
How to respond to borrowers across channels, what standards apply, and how to resolve common requests

Contact Channels and Response Standards

Channel What Borrowers Use It For Your Response Standard
Zype App Account details, EMI schedule, repayment, KFS download, complaints Real-time / automated; agent escalation within 24 hours
Helpline / Phone Loan queries, payment issues, complaints, KFS queries Answer within defined ring time; resolve or escalate within the call
Email Formal complaints, document requests, legal notices Acknowledge within 24 hours; resolve within 5 working days
In-app Chat Quick queries, payment assistance, app navigation help First response within defined SLA; escalate complex issues

The 4-Step Service Framework

  1. Greet and identify. Introduce yourself by name as a Respo Customer Service representative. Confirm the borrower's identity using the registered mobile number or loan account number.
  2. Listen and understand. Let the borrower fully explain their query or issue. Do not interrupt. Ask clarifying questions only after they have finished.
  3. Resolve or escalate. Provide the answer or solution if within your authority. If not, escalate immediately — do not keep the borrower waiting or promise what you cannot deliver.
  4. Confirm and close. Summarise the resolution, confirm next steps, and ask if the borrower has any further questions. Thank them for reaching out.

Common Service Requests and How to Handle Them

Borrower Request What to Do What Not to Do
"What is my outstanding amount?" Pull up the account and share the current outstanding principal, accrued interest, and any overdue charges Never give an approximate or estimated figure
"I did not receive my KFS" Inform borrower that KFS is part of loan agreement mailed on his registered email id. If required, we can resend again Do not refuse sending KFS to the borrower as already sent once
"My eNACH failed, EMI not deducted" Guide borrower to pay manually via Zype app or payment link. Confirm receipt of payment once completed. Do not tell the borrower to ignore a failed EMI
"I want to repay my loan early" Explain the prepayment/foreclosure process via the Zype app or approved channel. Check the borrower's KFS/loan agreement and confirm the exact amount and any applicable charges before advising the borrower. Do not discourage prepayment
"Can I get a top-up or increased limit?" Explain that any credit-limit increase is subject to credit assessment and requires the borrower's explicit consent to be taken on record for each increase. No automatic enhancement. Do not represent any limit increase as automatic. Follow the approved offer/consent process and ensure explicit borrower consent is taken on record for each increase.
"I did not authorise this loan" Treat as a dispute. Log immediately and escalate to supervisor. Do not continue collections on this account. Do not dismiss the claim or ask for payment

Real Call Scenarios

💬 Scenario A — Borrower does not understand their EMI amount
✗ Wrong: "The EMI is ₹X. It is all in your agreement. Check the app." (dismissive, no explanation)
✓ Right: "Thank you for calling. Your EMI of ₹X is made up of the principal repayment and the interest for that month. The interest rate on your loan is as stated in your Key Fact Statement. Would you like me to walk you through the KFS? I can also resend it to your registered email."
💬 Scenario B — Borrower says their eNACH failed and they are worried about penalty
✗ Wrong: "You should have made sure the account had funds. Penalty will be charged." (dismissive and does not verify the applicable contractual charge)
✓ Right: "I understand this can be stressful. Let me check your account. Your EMI of ₹X was due on [date] and the eNACH was unsuccessful. You can make the payment manually through the approved payment option, and I can guide you. I will also confirm the exact penal charge, if any, applicable to your loan as disclosed in your KFS/loan agreement."
Module 04
Grievance Handling and Escalation
Respo's internal grievance escalation structure, RBI requirements, response timelines, and how to log and escalate complaints

Every Borrower Has the Right to Complain

Under Respo's Fair Practices Code and the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, every borrower has access to a formal grievance redressal mechanism. You must never discourage, dismiss, or delay a borrower who wishes to lodge a complaint.

Respo's Internal Grievance Redressal Structure

Level Who Trigger Timeline
Level 1 Customer Service Team (you) First point of contact for all complaints Resolve within 5 working days
Level 2 Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO) Escalation if borrower is unsatisfied with Level 1 OR if unresolved after 5 days Respond within 15 working days of escalation
Level 3 RBI Ombudsman (cms.rbi.org.in) If Respo rejects the complaint wholly or partly, the borrower is dissatisfied with Respo's reply, or no reply is received within 30 days, subject to the applicable RBI Ombudsman framework RBI Ombudsman route applies subject to RB-IOS maintainability requirements; Respo must respond within the timeline specified by RBI.
RBI Ombudsman Escalation: If a complaint is rejected wholly or partly by Respo, the borrower is not satisfied with Respo's reply, or no reply is received within 30 days of receipt of the complaint by Respo, the borrower may lodge a complaint through RBI's Complaint Management System, subject to the applicable provisions of the Reserve Bank – Integrated Ombudsman Scheme. Log every complaint immediately with the date and time stamp.

How to Log a Complaint — Every Time

  1. Record the complaint in the CRM system immediately — not at the end of your shift.
  2. Capture: borrower name, loan account number, date and time, nature of complaint, channel (phone/email/app/chat), and any resolution offered.
  3. Assign the complaint a unique reference number and share it with the borrower.
  4. Set a follow-up reminder for 3 working days to check if the complaint is progressing toward resolution.
  5. If unresolved by day 4, escalate to the GRO — do not wait until day 5.

GRO Contact — What to Share with Borrowers

Mandatory: For digital lending, the contact details of the designated nodal grievance redressal officer must be prominently available on the websites of the RE/LSP, on the DLA and in the KFS. If a borrower asks for grievance contact details, provide the approved details immediately and do not delay or deflect.

When a Borrower Mentions the RBI Ombudsman

If a borrower says they will approach the RBI Ombudsman:

  • Acknowledge appropriately: "You may approach the RBI Ombudsman in accordance with the applicable RBI grievance redressal framework, and we will cooperate with any communication received from RBI."
  • Provide the RBI CMS portal link: cms.rbi.org.in
  • Inform your supervisor and the GRO immediately — RBI complaints have a mandatory response timeline.
  • Never try to dissuade the borrower from filing an RBI complaint.
💬 Scenario C — Borrower is unhappy with complaint resolution and threatens to go to RBI
✗ Wrong: "That is your choice, but our decision stands." (dismissive, unhelpful)
✓ Right: "I understand you are not satisfied with the resolution and I am sorry for that. I will escalate your case to our Grievance Redressal Officer. You may also approach the RBI Ombudsman through RBI's Complaint Management System in accordance with the applicable grievance redressal framework."
Module 05
Cooling-Off Period and Loan Cancellations
What the cooling-off period is, how borrowers can exercise it, and your responsibilities

What is the Cooling-Off Period?

The cooling-off period is a borrower's right to cancel a loan after disbursement — without any penalty — by repaying the principal amount and the proportionate APR for the days the loan was outstanding.

RBI Requirement: Under the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, the cooling-off/look-up period is determined by the Board of the RE. It must be at least three days for loans having a tenor of seven days or more and at least one day for loans having a tenor of less than seven days. Respo's approved product process must be followed where it provides a longer period.

How the Cooling-Off Period Works

Question Answer
Who can use it? Borrowers of digital loans, subject to the applicable KFS/product terms
How long is the period? As stated in the applicable KFS/Board-approved product policy, subject to the RBI minimum
What must be repaid? Principal + proportionate APR for the period the loan was held. A reasonable one-time processing fee may be retained only if disclosed upfront in the KFS and permitted under the applicable framework.
How does the borrower exercise it? Through the Zype app or by contacting Customer Service
What happens after repayment? The loan is cancelled, the agreement is terminated, and a confirmation is sent to the borrower
Can Respo charge a penalty? No penalty for exercising the cooling-off exit option. Any one-time processing fee retained must have been disclosed upfront in the KFS and be permitted under the applicable RBI framework.
Critical: Never discourage a borrower from exercising their cooling-off right. Never tell a borrower they "cannot" exit the loan during the applicable cooling-off period. Follow the Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025, the borrower's KFS/loan agreement and Respo's Board-approved policy.

Your Role When a Borrower Requests Cancellation

  1. Confirm the applicable cooling-off/look-up period from the borrower's KFS/product configuration and check the disbursement date.
  2. Explain what they need to repay: principal + proportionate APR for the days held. Calculate and confirm the exact amount.
  3. Guide the borrower to make the repayment via the Zype app or bank transfer.
  4. Once the applicable exit amount is received, process the cooling-off exit in accordance with the approved procedure and issue written confirmation.
  5. Log the entire interaction in the CRM system with timestamps.

No Automatic Credit Enhancements

Important Rule: Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 explicitly prohibit automatic loan enhancements or credit limit increases without the explicit written request of the borrower. If a borrower contacts you to ask why their credit limit increased without their knowledge — treat this as a Priority 1 complaint and escalate immediately.

What You Must Never Do
Prohibited practices under RBI guidelines — zero tolerance
Zero Tolerance: Respo has a zero tolerance policy for any practice that harms, misleads, or manipulates borrowers. Violations will be investigated and may result in disciplinary action including termination and reporting to the regulator.

Prohibited Practices — No Exceptions

Prohibited Practice Regulatory Basis
Misrepresenting loan terms, APR, or fees to a borrower Reserve Bank of India (Non-Banking Financial Companies – Responsible Business Conduct) Directions, 2025 + Respo Fair Practices Code
Not delivering or delaying delivery of the KFS Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025
Increasing a borrower's credit limit without their explicit request Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025
Discouraging or obstructing a borrower from exercising cooling-off right Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025
Using dark patterns — confusing UI, hidden charges, forced opt-ins CCPA Guidelines on Prevention and Regulation of Dark Patterns, 2023 + applicable RBI directions
Sharing borrower personal data without consent or for non-approved purposes Applicable data-protection law / Respo policy
Making false or misleading statements about consequences of non-payment Respo Fair Practices Code / applicable RBI directions
Accessing mobile contacts, call logs, files/media or telephony functions through the DLA; or accessing camera/microphone/location for onboarding/KYC without explicit consent Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025

Contacting a borrower outside permitted channels or without consent Applicable privacy/data-protection requirements + RBI directions

Dark Patterns — What Are They and Why Are They Prohibited?

Dark patterns are design or communication techniques that trick or manipulate borrowers into doing something they did not intend. The RBI and CCPA Guidelines 2023 explicitly prohibit them.

Dark Pattern Example in Lending Context
Hidden charges Showing a lower processing fee on screen but charging a higher amount at disbursement
Forced opt-in Pre-ticking a box for a borrower to receive marketing calls - consent must be explicit
Misleading APR Showing monthly interest rate prominently but hiding the annual APR
Urgency pressure "Offer expires in 2 minutes” - creating artificial urgency to force a loan decision
Difficult cancellation Making it hard to find the cancellation or cooling-off option in the app

Do's and Don'ts — Customer Communication

✓ WHAT YOU SHOULD DO ✗ WHAT YOU MUST NEVER DO
✓ Explain all fees, charges and APR clearly and completely ✗ Quote only the monthly interest rate without mentioning APR
✓ Provide KFS proactively and confirm borrower has received it ✗ Assume the borrower has read or understood the KFS
✓ Offer GRO contact at the borrower's first sign of dissatisfaction ✗ Wait for the borrower to demand GRO contact
✓ Use plain language — avoid jargon and acronyms without explanation ✗ Use technical terms without explaining them
✓ Confirm all commitments made to the borrower in writing ✗ Make verbal promises you cannot fulfil
Module 07
Data Privacy and Confidentiality
Your obligations under RBI digital-lending data requirements, applicable data-protection law and Respo's data policy

Borrower Data Must Be Protected

Borrower information such as name, mobile number, address, employer, income details, loan amount, repayment history and bank-account details must be handled only for authorised purposes and in accordance with Respo's privacy and information-security policies and applicable law.

Strict Rule: You may use borrower data only for authorised business purposes connected with your role. Unauthorised access, use, storage or disclosure may violate Respo's policies and applicable law and may result in disciplinary and/or regulatory consequences.

What You Must Never Do with Borrower Data

  • Share a borrower's personal or account information with anyone other than the borrower themselves (or their authorized representative)
  • Save borrower data on your personal phone, personal email, or any unapproved device or platform
  • Screenshot or photograph your CRM screen on a personal device
  • Discuss borrower account details with colleagues who do not need to know
  • Share any borrower information on social media, WhatsApp groups, or any external platform
  • Access borrower accounts that are not part of your work queue or assigned cases

Borrower Data Requests — How to Handle Them

Borrower Data Request Your Responsibility
Request to know/access personal data Follow the approved privacy/data-request process or escalate to the GRO/Privacy team; do not provide an unverified data inventory.
Request to correct/update personal or KYC data Log and escalate the request. Do not manually overwrite verified KYC. Where re-KYC/re-verification is required, guide the borrower through the approved process so updated particulars are fetched/verified through the authorised KYC flow.
Data privacy complaint Log immediately and provide/escalate to the designated grievance/privacy contact as per the approved process.
Request to withdraw marketing consent / communication preference Process or escalate promptly through the approved communication-preference/consent process.

RBI Rules on Data Collection

The Reserve Bank of India (Digital Lending) Directions, 2025 place specific restrictions on data collection by digital lending apps. If a borrower raises concerns about data collected by the Zype app, you must know these rules:

  • Data collection must be purpose-specific and consent-based
  • The DLA must not access mobile-phone resources such as files/media, contact lists, call logs or telephony functions. One-time access may be taken, with the borrower's explicit consent, only for camera, microphone, location or another facility necessary for onboarding/KYC.
  • Data covered by the Digital Lending Directions must be stored only on servers located in India. If such data is processed outside India, it must be deleted from servers outside India and brought back to India within 24 hours of processing, subject to applicable statutory/regulatory requirements.
  • Personal information must not be shared with third parties without the borrower's explicit consent, except where sharing is required under applicable statutory or regulatory requirements.

If You Suspect a Data Breach

  1. Do not try to handle it yourself or cover it up.
  2. Report it to your supervisor and the Compliance team immediately — use the data breach reporting procedure.
  3. Immediately inform your supervisor, Information Security and Compliance teams. They will determine and complete any notification/reporting required under the laws and regulatory directions applicable at the time — so timing and accurate escalation matter.
  4. Log the incident with full details including what you observed, when, and who you informed.
Module 08
Handling Difficult Situations
How to manage distressed borrowers, disputes, and sensitive or escalated scenarios

Borrower is Distressed or Emotional

Digital lending customers may contact us at stressful moments — when they cannot make a payment, are facing personal hardship, or feel misled. Your tone and approach in these moments define the borrower's experience of Respo.

  1. Stay calm. Your tone sets the tone for the entire interaction.
  2. Acknowledge the feeling first before moving to facts: "I understand this must be frustrating for you."
  3. Do not rush. Let the borrower finish before you respond.
  4. Focus on what you CAN do, not what you cannot.
  5. If the situation is beyond your authority, escalate — and tell the borrower clearly what will happen next and when.
💬 Scenario D — Borrower is crying and says they cannot afford to pay
✗ Wrong: "I understand but you need to make the payment. That is what you signed up for." (clinical, unhelpful)
✓ Right: "I can hear that you're going through a really difficult time and I want to help. Let me note your situation on the account right now and escalate it to our team that handles hardship cases. They will reach out to you within [timeframe]. In the meantime, would even a small payment today help reduce the charges accumulating on your account? I can help you with that right now if you'd like."

Borrower Disputes the Loan — Says They Did Not Authorize It

  1. Do not dismiss the claim. Take it seriously and treat it as a priority dispute.
  2. Tell the borrower: "I understand. I am logging this as a formal dispute right now and escalating it for urgent review."
  3. Escalate to your supervisor and the compliance team immediately.
  4. Do not continue any collections or payment follow-up on a disputed account without explicit supervisor instruction.
  5. Issue a dispute acknowledgement to the borrower with a reference number and the expected review timeline.

Borrower Threatens Legal Action or Media

If a borrower says they will file a police complaint, approach a consumer court, contact media, or post on social media:

  • Stay calm. Do not panic, become defensive, or make counter-threats.
  • Respond: "You are absolutely within your rights to take any action you feel is appropriate. We want to resolve this for you. Let me connect you with our Grievance Redressal Officer immediately."
  • Escalate to your supervisor and the GRO without delay.
  • Do not make any promises or statements about legal outcomes — that is not your role.

Borrower Makes a Complaint About a Respo Staff Member

If a borrower complains about the behaviour of a Respo employee (including another customer service agent or a collections agent):

  • Take the complaint seriously — do not be dismissive or defensive on behalf of the colleague.
  • Log the complaint immediately with full details.
  • Escalate to your supervisor and the GRO — this is a formal complaint that requires investigation.
  • Assure the borrower that the complaint will be investigated and they will receive a response within the standard timeline.
Reminder: All interactions with borrowers are subject to monitoring. Your calls may be recorded. Ensure you maintain the same standard of conduct whether or not you think you are being observed.
Module 09
Credit Information Reporting and Bureau Disputes
What Customer Service Must Know

All digital loans must be reported to Credit Information Companies (CICs) in accordance with the applicable RBI credit-information framework, irrespective of the nature or tenor of the digital credit.

Reporting frequency: credit information is to be kept updated on a fortnightly basis (as on the 15th and the last day of the month) or at shorter intervals agreed with the CIC, with submission within the prescribed RBI timeline.

When a borrower disputes bureau information:

  1. Log the dispute immediately and identify the exact loan/tradeline, field and reporting period disputed.
  2. Verify the lender's source records before confirming whether the bureau information is correct or requires rectification.
  3. If correction is required, escalate to the authorised bureau-reporting team. Customer Service must not manually alter bureau data.
  4. Communicate the action taken to the borrower. If a correction request is rejected, provide the reason for rejection.
  5. Credit-information correction complaints have a regulatory resolution framework of 30 calendar days overall. Where delay attributable to the CI/CIC exceeds the prescribed period, compensation of ₹100 per calendar day may become applicable under the RBI framework.
Important: Do not promise an immediate bureau-score increase. Respo can correct/report underlying account information where required; the credit score itself is generated by the CIC.
Module 10
Recovery Conduct and Customer Protection
Handling recovery-related complaints promptly, factually and without defensiveness

Recovery-related complaints are high-risk complaints and must be handled promptly, factually and without defensiveness.

Core rules:

  • No intimidation, threats, humiliation, abusive language or misleading representations.
  • Do not persistently call a borrower. Recovery calls for overdue loans must not be made before 8:00 a.m. or after 7:00 p.m.
  • Do not intrude upon the privacy of family members, referees or friends, and do not disclose the borrower's debt to unauthorised third parties.
  • When a recovery agent is assigned or changed for a defaulted digital loan, the authorised agent's particulars must be communicated to the borrower by email/SMS before the agent contacts the borrower.
  • Recovery agents must carry/possess the required notice, authorisation and identification as applicable, and the NBFC remains responsible for outsourced recovery conduct.
  • Any complaint alleging threats, excessive calls, third-party disclosure, workplace pressure, abusive communication or unauthorised field conduct must be logged and escalated for review.
Customer Service must never defend an agent before reviewing records. Acknowledge the concern, verify call/communication logs, escalate where required, and communicate the outcome appropriately.
Module 11
Accessibility and Non-Discrimination
Providing fair access and reasonable assistance to all customers

RBI requires NBFCs not to discriminate in extending products and facilities, including loan facilities, to physically or visually challenged applicants on grounds of disability. Reasonable assistance must be provided for access to services.

Customer Service responsibilities:

  • Treat customers with disabilities with dignity and without assumptions about their ability to understand or use a product.
  • Provide reasonable assistance through available accessible channels and escalate accessibility barriers promptly.
  • Do not refuse or discourage a loan/service request merely because a customer is physically or visually challenged.
  • Accessibility-related grievances must be handled through the established grievance redressal mechanism.
