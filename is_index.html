Why Information Security Matters
Understanding the threat landscape, regulatory obligations, and your personal responsibility

The Threat Landscape

Respo is a fully digital lender. Every borrower interaction, every loan, every KYC document exists in digital systems. This makes us a target.

Threat What It Is Risk to Respo
Phishing Fake emails/messages tricking you into revealing passwords or clicking malicious links Credential theft, account takeover, data breach
Ransomware Malware that encrypts all systems and demands payment Complete shutdown, data loss, RBI notification required
Social Engineering Manipulating employees into giving away information or access Unauthorised access, fraud, regulatory penalty
Insider Threat Employees misusing access to data or systems — intentionally or accidentally Data leak, DPDP penalty, borrower harm
Third-Party Risk Vendors with access to our systems being compromised Supply-chain breach affecting Respo data

Our Regulatory Framework

Regulation Key Obligation
RBI IT MD 2023 Board-approved IS Policy; annual IS Audit by CERT-In empanelled auditor; incident reporting; access controls; data classification
CERT-In Directions April 2022 Report qualifying cyber incidents within 6 hours of identification; maintain logs for 180 days minimum
DPDP Act 2023 Protect borrower personal data; implement security safeguards; notify Data Protection Board of breaches within prescribed timeline
RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025 All borrower data stored in India; no access to mobile contacts/call logs without consent; purpose-limited data collection
Important: Information security is not just the IT team's job. Every employee — from CEO to new joiner — has a personal obligation to protect our systems, data, and borrowers. You are Respo's first line of defence.

Your Specific Obligations

  • Follow Respo's IS Policy and all security guidelines at all times
  • Complete this annual security awareness training and pass the assessment
  • Report any suspected security incident immediately — no matter how minor
  • Never share your login credentials with anyone including your manager or IT support
  • Lock your screen whenever you leave your workstation — even for a minute
  • Keep software and systems updated when prompted by IT
  • Treat all borrower data as confidential
Module 02
Protecting Your Passwords and Accounts
Password rules, multi-factor authentication, and keeping your accounts secure

Password Requirements

Mandatory: Respo's IS Policy, aligned with RBI IT MD 2023, requires all employees to follow these mandatory password rules.
Rule Detail
Minimum length 12 characters for standard accounts; longer is always better
Complexity Must include uppercase, lowercase, numbers, AND at least one special character (@, #, !, $)
Change frequency Every 90 days for standard accounts; every 30 days for privileged/admin accounts
No reuse Cannot reuse your last 5 passwords
No sharing Never share with anyone — including IT support, your manager, or a covering colleague
No personal info Do not use your name, date of birth, or "Respo" in your password

Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA)

Mandatory: Under RBI IT MD 2023, MFA is mandatory for all privileged accounts and remote access sessions. MFA adds a second verification step — even if someone steals your password, they cannot log in without the second factor.
  • OTP sent to your registered mobile number/email Id
  • Authenticator app (Google Authenticator, Microsoft Authenticator)
  • Web Authentication (origin binding)
  • Hardware token (for privileged/admin accounts)
Warning: If you receive an MFA prompt that you did NOT initiate — someone is attempting to access your account. Deny immediately and report to IT security.

Strong vs. Weak Passwords

✓ STRONG PASSWORDS

  • G3st0@Secur3!2026 — long, complex, memorable phrase
  • Tr08z#Digital$Lend!ng — passphrase-style
  • Use a password manager to generate and store complex passwords
  • Change your password immediately if you suspect compromise

✗ WEAK PASSWORDS

  • Password123 — too simple and common
  • Your name + birth year — easily guessable
  • Using the same password across multiple systems
  • Writing passwords on sticky notes at your desk

Real Scenarios

⚠ Scenario A — Unexpected MFA Prompt
✗ Wrong:
You receive an MFA approval notification on your phone even though you are not trying to log in. You assume it is an IT activity and approve the request.
✓ Right:
Deny the request immediately. Do not approve any MFA request you did not initiate. Change your password using the approved process and report the incident to IT Security immediately. An unexpected MFA prompt may mean that someone already has your password and is attempting to access your account.
⚠ Scenario B — You Think Your Password Has Been Compromised
✗ Wrong:
You accidentally enter your Respo username and password on a suspicious website. Since nothing happens immediately, you close the website and continue working without reporting it.
✓ Right:
Report the incident to IT Security immediately and change your password using the approved process. Do not wait to see whether your account is misused. If the same or similar password has been used on any other approved system, follow IT Security's instructions to secure those accounts as well.
⚠ Scenario C — Colleague Asks to Use Your Login
✗ Wrong:
You give your username and password because it is faster than waiting for IT to grant access.
✓ Right:
Politely decline: "Sorry, I cannot share my login — it is against our security policy and I am personally accountable for everything done under my credentials. Let me help you raise an IT access request instead."
Module 03
Phishing and Social Engineering
How attackers try to manipulate you — and how to recognise and respond

What Is Phishing?

Phishing is the most common cyberattack targeting organisations like Respo. Attackers send fake emails, SMS, or make phone calls pretending to be someone trustworthy — to trick you into clicking malicious links, downloading malware, or revealing sensitive information.

Attack Type How It Works Common Disguise
Email Phishing Fake email with malicious link or attachment RBI circular, bank alert, IT helpdesk, HR
Spear Phishing Targeted email crafted specifically for you or Respo CEO asking for urgent payment, "your manager"
Smishing (SMS) Fake SMS with malicious link OTP alert, bank notification, delivery update
Vishing (Voice) Phone call pretending to be IT support, RBI, or a vendor "Your account is compromised — give us your OTP"
CEO Fraud / BEC Urgent financial request pretending to be senior management WhatsApp from "CEO", email from spoofed domain

Red Flags — How to Spot a Phishing Attempt

  • Sender email address does not match the claimed organisation — e.g. rbi-alert@gmail.com
  • Urgent language — "Act immediately or your account will be suspended"
  • Generic greeting — "Dear User" instead of your name
  • Spelling and grammar mistakes
  • Suspicious link — hover over it (without clicking) to see the actual URL destination
  • Unexpected attachment — especially .exe, .zip, .doc with macros
  • Request for your password, OTP, or banking credentials
  • Unusual payment request from someone claiming to be senior management
Critical Rule: Respo's IT team and senior management will NEVER ask for your password, OTP, or banking credentials via email or phone. If anyone asks — it is an attack. Report immediately.

Real Scenarios

⚠ Scenario D — Fake RBI Email
✗ Wrong:
You receive an email from rbi.compliance@gmail.com with an attachment titled "RBI Circular — Urgent Action Required." You open it.
✓ Right:
Stop. RBI never sends circulars from Gmail addresses. Do not open attachments. Forward the email to IT security and delete it from your inbox.
⚠ Scenario E — Fake IT Helpdesk Call
✗ Wrong:
A caller says: "This is Respo IT support. We detected a breach on your account. Please share your OTP to secure it." You share the OTP.
✓ Right:
Hang up. IT support will never ask for your OTP. Call the IT team on the official internal number to verify. Report the call to IT security immediately.
⚠ Scenario F — CEO Payment Fraud
✗ Wrong:
You receive a WhatsApp from a number claiming to be the CEO: "Urgent — process a payment of Rs 50,000 to this account. Confidential." You process it.
✓ Right:
Stop. This is CEO fraud / BEC. Always verify urgent financial requests by calling the person directly on their known official number — not the same channel the request came from. Then report to IT security.

What to Do If You Suspect Phishing

  1. Do not click any links or open attachments.
  2. Do not reply to the message.
  3. Forward to IT security at infosec@Respo.co.in and report via the incident process.
  4. Delete the email from your inbox and sent folder.
  5. If you accidentally clicked — report immediately, do not wait.
Module 04
Safe Use of Devices and Systems
Protecting Respo equipment, working remotely, and handling removable media

Respo-Issued Devices

Any device issued by Respo — laptop, mobile phone, tablet — is company property for Respo business only:

  • Do not install unauthorised software on Respo devices
  • Do not connect to unknown or public Wi-Fi without a VPN
  • Do not use Respo devices for personal file storage, social media, or personal shopping
  • Do not leave Respo devices unattended in public places
  • Enable device encryption on all Respo devices — confirm with IT
Lost/Stolen Device: If a Respo device is lost or stolen — report to IT security IMMEDIATELY.

Personal Devices — BYOD Rules

  • Only access Respo systems through approved secure channels — VPN, Respo-approved apps
  • Do not store Respo data — including borrower data — on your personal device
  • Do not forward Respo emails to your personal email account
  • Keep your personal device OS and apps updated
  • Please update the apps/OS as and when recommended by the IT team on priority.
  • Enable screen lock with PIN or biometric on your personal device

Remote Working Security

Rule Why It Matters
Always use approved VPN when accessing Respo systems remotely Public networks can be intercepted — VPN encrypts your connection
Make sure that MFA is configured on your VPN MFA makes sure that no anonymous person is able to connect to VPN.
Never work from a public or shared computer Your credentials and session data can be captured
Position your screen so others cannot see it in public Visual shoulder-surfing is a real threat in cafes and airports
Lock your screen before stepping away even at home Family members should not have access to Respo systems
Ensure home Wi-Fi uses WPA2/WPA3 encryption Unsecured home networks can be exploited

Removable Media — USB Drives

Critical: Never plug an unknown USB drive into a Respo system. Attackers deliberately leave infected drives in public places and offices. This is a "USB drop attack."
  • Only use Respo-approved encrypted removable media when absolutely necessary
  • Do not use personal USB drives on Respo systems
  • Do not take borrower data off Respo systems on removable media without written authorisation
  • If you find an unknown USB drive — hand it to IT security. Do not plug it in "to see what is on it."
Module 05
Data Classification and Handling
Understanding what data we hold, how it is classified, and how to handle it safely

Respo Data Classification Framework

Level What It Includes Who Can Access Key Rules
RESTRICTED Borrower personal data, KYC, loan details, system passwords, security keys Need-to-know only Encrypt in transit and at rest; never email unencrypted; never share without authorisation
CONFIDENTIAL Internal business data, employee records, financial reports, vendor contracts, IT security reports Respo employees with business need Do not share externally without approval; store in authorised systems only
INTERNAL General internal communications, policies, training materials All Respo employees Do not share publicly; standard access controls apply
PUBLIC Respo website content, Zype app, regulatory disclosures Everyone No restrictions on sharing
Highest Protection: Borrower data is RESTRICTED — the highest protection level. It is also personal data under the DPDP Act 2023. A breach can result in regulatory penalties, legal action, reputation loss, and serious harm to borrowers.

Rules for Handling Borrower (Restricted) Data

  • Access only the borrower data you need for your specific work
  • Never copy borrower data to personal devices, personal email, or WhatsApp
  • Never share borrower data in unencrypted email
  • Never discuss borrower account details where others can overhear
  • Ensure your screen is not visible to others when accessing borrower accounts
  • Log out of all Respo systems before leaving your workstation

Data Disposal — When Data Is No Longer Needed

  • Digital files — delete from all systems and empty recycle bin; ask IT if secure deletion tools are required
  • Printed documents — shred all documents containing confidential data; never place in regular waste bins
  • Old devices — hand to IT for secure data wiping before disposal

Safe vs. Unsafe Data Sharing

✓ SAFE DATA SHARING

  • Use Respo-approved secure file sharing systems
  • Password-protect files containing sensitive data before sharing
  • Verify recipient identity before sharing confidential data
  • Seek manager approval before sharing data with third parties

✗ UNSAFE DATA SHARING

  • Emailing borrower data as an unencrypted attachment
  • Sharing borrower screenshots on WhatsApp
  • Sending confidential data to a wrong email address
  • Uploading borrower data to personal cloud storage
Module 06
Malware and Ransomware Protection
Understanding malicious software, how to avoid it, and what to do if infected

Types of Malware

Type What It Does How It Spreads
Ransomware Encrypts all your files and demands payment — can shut down the entire organisation Phishing emails, malicious downloads, unpatched systems
Virus Attaches to files and replicates, corrupting data Infected files via email, USB, or downloads
Trojan Disguises itself as legitimate software — opens backdoors for attackers Downloaded from unofficial sources, fake software updates
Spyware Silently monitors your activity, capturing passwords and keystrokes Bundled with free software, malicious websites
Keylogger Records every keystroke — capturing passwords and confidential data Installed via phishing or physical device tampering

How to Protect Yourself

  • Never download software from unofficial or unknown websites — IT-approved software only
  • Never open email attachments from unknown senders — especially .exe, .zip, .doc, .sh files
  • Never click on pop-up alerts saying "Your computer has a virus — click here"
  • Keep OS and software updated on priority — apply patches promptly when IT notifies you
  • Never disable antivirus or security software even temporarily
  • Never plug in unknown USB drives
Highest Risk: Ransomware can encrypt every file on Respo's systems within minutes of a single employee clicking a malicious link. RBI and CERT-In must be notified. Recovery can take weeks and cost crores.

Signs of Malware Infection — Report Immediately if You See

  • Files with strange new extensions (.locked, .encrypted, .crypto)
  • Files that previously worked cannot be opened
  • A ransom demand message appears on your screen
  • Computer significantly slower than usual without explanation
  • Files or folders disappearing or being renamed automatically

If You Suspect Malware

  1. Disconnect from the network IMMEDIATELY — unplug ethernet or disable Wi-Fi. Do NOT shut down the computer.
  2. Do not attempt to fix it yourself.
  3. Call IT security immediately on +919902361234 or +919940509494.
  4. Do not use any other Respo system until IT clears you.
  5. Preserve all evidence — do not delete any files or logs (required for Malware Analysis in Forensic audits)
Good to Know: Why disconnect but NOT shut down? Shutting down an infected device destroys forensic evidence needed to identify the attacker and recover data. Disconnecting from the network stops the malware spreading to other systems.
Module 07
Data Privacy and DPDP Act 2023
Your legal obligations under India's data protection law and how to protect borrower rights

What Is the DPDP Act 2023?

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 is India's primary data protection law. It regulates how organisations like Respo collect, use, store, and share personal data of individuals — including our borrowers and employees.

Your Legal Obligation: As a Respo employee, you are a "person who processes data" under the DPDP Act. This means you have legal obligations — not just policy obligations — to protect personal data. Violations can result in penalties of up to Rs 250 crore for Respo.

Your 5 Core Obligations Under DPDP Act 2023

  1. Purpose limitation: Use personal data only for the specific purpose it was collected for. Do not repurpose borrower data for anything other than your assigned work.
  2. Data minimisation: Access only the data you need for your specific task. Do not collect more than necessary.
  3. Security safeguards: Protect personal data using the technical and organisational measures in this manual.
  4. Breach reporting: If you suspect or discover a data breach — report it immediately. Any breach MUST be reported to the Data Protection Board within 6 hours so time is extremely critical.
  5. Borrower rights: If a borrower raises a data rights request — escalate to the Compliance team immediately.

Borrower Data Rights

Right Your Responsibility
Right to know what data we hold Direct borrower to Zype app privacy section or GRO contact — do not handle yourself
Right to correct inaccurate data Log correction request and escalate to Compliance team — do not correct data yourself
Right to raise a data complaint Provide GRO contact details and log the complaint immediately
Right to withdraw consent for marketing Process immediately — update communication preferences in the system
Right to data erasure (where applicable) Escalate to Compliance team — regulatory retention requirements may apply
RBI Data Localisation: All borrower data and payment data collected by Respo must be stored in India. This is a mandatory requirement under RBI Digital Lending Directions 2025. If any vendor or tool stores data outside India — raise it with IT and Compliance immediately.
Module 08
Incident Reporting and Response
What counts as a security incident, how to report it, and the CERT-In 6-hour rule

What Is a Security Incident?

A security incident is any event that could compromise the confidentiality, integrity, or availability of Respo's systems or data. If in doubt — report it. It is always better to report and be wrong than to not report and be right.

Incident Type Examples
Phishing / Social Engineering Suspicious email received; phishing link clicked; OTP shared with unauthorised person
Malware / Ransomware Unusual system behaviour; files encrypting; ransom demand on screen
Unauthorised Access Someone logging into your account without your knowledge
Data Breach Borrower data sent to wrong recipient; data found on public platform; data accessed without authorisation
Lost or Stolen Device Respo laptop, phone, or token lost or stolen
System Anomaly Unexpected slowdown; files missing or renamed; unusual network activity
Golden Rule: Report FIRST. Investigate LATER. Never delay reporting because you are not sure it is real, because you are embarrassed, or because you think you can fix it yourself.

How to Report a Security Incident

  1. Step 1: Immediately contact IT Security: Call +919940509494 or email infosec@Respo.co.in — available 24/7.
  2. Step 2: Preserve evidence: Do not delete emails, files, or logs. Do not shut down an infected device. Disconnect from network if malware is suspected.
  3. Step 3: Document what you observed: Time, what you saw, what you did, which systems were involved.
  4. Step 4: Inform your supervisor: Your manager MUST be notified for any security incident.
  5. Step 5: Cooperate fully with the IT security investigation. Do not discuss the incident beyond those who need to know.

Regulatory Reporting Timelines — Why Speed Matters

Regulator Timeline What Must Be Reported
CERT-In 6 hours from identification Qualifying cyber incidents per CERT-In Directions April 2022 — ransomware, data breaches, phishing attacks on critical systems
RBI Per RBI IT MD 2023 timelines Material cyber incidents affecting Respo's systems or customer data
Data Protection Board Within prescribed timeline under DPDP Act 2023 Any personal data breach affecting borrower or employee data
Why 6 Hours Matters: The 6-hour CERT-In deadline is extremely tight. Every employee must report incidents immediately — the IT team needs time to assess, contain, and report. A delayed employee report can cause Respo to miss the CERT-In deadline.
Respo's Policy: No employee will be penalised for reporting a genuine security incident in good faith — even if it turns out to be a false alarm. What is not acceptable is knowing about an incident and not reporting it.
