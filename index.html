Module 01
Who We Are and What We Do
Understanding Respo's mission and how the collections team fits in.

About Respo Financial Capital

Respo Financial Capital Private Limited is a Reserve Bank of India (RBI) registered Non-Banking Financial Company (NBFC). We provide unsecured personal loans to individuals through the Zype app, operated by Easy Platform Services Private Limited.

Our loans range from 6 to 36 months in tenure and are disbursed entirely digitally — no physical branches, no cash. Everything happens through the Zype app.

Our Promise to Every Borrower: Respo is committed to providing credit in a fair, transparent, and responsible manner. This is not just a value — it is a legal obligation under the RBI Responsible Business Conduct Directions 2025.

Our Regulatory Framework

As an RBI-regulated NBFC, Respo must follow a strict set of rules when dealing with borrowers — especially when it comes to recovering dues. The key regulations that govern your work are:

Regulation What It Covers
RBI RBC-MD 2025 Fair treatment of borrowers, prohibited recovery practices, grievance redressal
RBI RBC Third Amendment 2026 Specific rules for recovery agents, calling hours, IIBF certification, pre-visit notification
Respo Fair Practice Code Our own commitment to borrowers — published on our website
Respo Code of Conduct (Collections) Your specific do's and don'ts as a collections team member
DPDP Act 2023 Rules on protecting borrower personal data

The Collections Team's Place at Respo

The collections team is one of the most important customer-facing functions at Respo. How you speak to and treat borrowers directly shapes:

  • The borrower's experience with Respo
  • Respo's reputation and RBI compliance
  • Our ability to recover dues sustainably
Remember: A borrower who is treated fairly and with dignity is far more likely to repay than one who feels harassed or threatened. Fair collections is also effective collections.
Module 02
Your Role in Collections
What you are responsible for, and how the collections process works at Respo.

In-House vs. Agency Collections

At Respo, collections is split into two tracks:

Track Who Handles It Bucket
Early Bucket (0–60 DPD) In-house team (you) Soft reminders, payment arrangements
Higher Bucket (60+ DPD / NPA) Empanelled Recovery Agencies Escalated recovery

As a member of the in-house collections team, you handle the early stages — where the relationship with the borrower is most important. Your goal is to help the borrower get back on track, not to threaten or pressure them.

Key Definitions You Need to Know

Term Meaning
DPD Days Past Due — number of days since the EMI was due but not paid
NPA Non-Performing Asset — a loan classified as bad debt (90 DPD)
EMI Equated Monthly Instalment — the fixed amount the borrower pays each month
Overdue Interest Interest charged on unpaid EMI amounts — at the same contracted rate, not an additional charge
Late Payment Penalty A capped daily penalty (max ₹2,500 per loan) for delayed payments
GRO Grievance Redressal Officer — the person a borrower can complain to
Penal Charges Late Payment Penalties — these are NOT interest and must NOT be capitalised

Your Core Objectives

Step Objective Description
1 Contact the borrower promptly Reach out as soon as an EMI is missed, using approved channels and timing.
2 Understand the borrower's situation Find out why the EMI was missed. Genuine financial distress is different from avoidance.
3 Offer a resolution path Payment within the current cycle, a part payment, or escalation to a supervisor for a formal arrangement.
4 Document every interaction Log all calls, SMS, and outcomes in the collections system immediately after each interaction.
5 Escalate when needed If the borrower is unresponsive or the account crosses 60 DPD, escalate to your supervisor for handoff to the agency track.
Your identity card: You must always identify yourself by name and as a representative of Respo Financial Capital at the start of every call. Never call from personal numbers — use only Respo-registered contact numbers.
Module 03
Rules of Engagement
The specific rules governing when, how, and who you can contact. These are RBI-mandated — not optional.

Calling Hours

You may only contact borrowers between 8:00 AM and 7:00 PM. Calls, SMS, WhatsApp messages, or any other form of contact outside these hours is strictly prohibited — regardless of how urgent the case is.

This rule applies to:

  • Phone calls
  • SMS messages
  • WhatsApp messages
  • Any other digital communication

Who You Can Contact

You may ONLY contact the borrower and/or the guarantor. You must NEVER discuss the borrower's loan dues with anyone else — not family members, not employers, not friends, not references — unless they are named as a guarantor in the loan agreement.

Contacting third parties to discuss a borrower's dues is a serious violation of RBI guidelines and the borrower's privacy rights under the DPDP Act 2023.

Number of Contacts

You must not make excessive calls. The following is prohibited:

  • Calling the same borrower multiple times in a day without a genuine reason
  • Sending more than the number of SMS/messages approved by your supervisor
  • Calling a borrower who has told you to stop calling and the matter is being handled (follow escalation procedure in that case)

Identification on Every Call

At the start of every call, you must:

  1. State your full name clearly
  2. State that you are calling on behalf of Respo Financial Capital Private Limited
  3. State the purpose of the call (EMI follow-up)
  4. Confirm you are speaking with the borrower (never assume)
📞 Sample Call Opening
"Good morning. Am I speaking with [Borrower Name]? This is [Your Name] calling from Respo Financial Capital. I am calling regarding your Zype loan account. Is this a good time to speak?"

Using Only Registered Numbers

All borrower contacts must be made only from Respo-registered phone numbers. Using your personal mobile number to contact a borrower is:

  • A violation of Respo's Code of Conduct
  • A potential violation of the borrower's privacy rights
  • Grounds for disciplinary action

Call Recording

All calls are recorded. Under the RBI RBC Third Amendment 2026, Respo is required to maintain recordings of all borrower calls for a minimum of 6 months. You must inform the borrower that the call is being recorded at the start of every call.
Module 04
What You Must Never Do
Prohibited practices under RBI guidelines and Respo's Code of Conduct. Violating these rules will result in immediate disciplinary action.
Zero tolerance policy: Respo has a zero tolerance approach to harsh or unethical recovery practices. Any violation of the rules in this module will be investigated and may result in termination and reporting to RBI.

Absolutely Prohibited — No Exceptions

Prohibited Practice Why It Is Prohibited
Using abusive, threatening, or obscene language Violation of RBI RBC-MD 2025 + Respo Code of Conduct
Threatening physical harm or any other harm Criminal offence + RBI violation
Calling outside 8 AM – 7 PM RBI RBC Third Amendment 2026 — explicit mandatory rule
Making anonymous or disguised calls RBI violation + TRAI regulations
Contacting employer, family, or friends about the loan Violation of DPDP Act 2023 + RBI FPC
Posting borrower details on social media or any public platform Serious RBI violation + criminal liability
Making false statements about legal consequences Misrepresentation — RBI FPC violation
Impersonating a court official, police officer, or government authority Criminal offence under IPC
Collecting cash from the borrower personally Respo does not accept cash — all payments via Zype app/bank transfer only
Excessive or repeated calling to harass RBI RBC-MD 2025 + TRAI

✓ WHAT YOU SHOULD SAY

  • "I understand your situation. Can we discuss a way to resolve this?"
  • "Your payment of ₹X is overdue. Would you like to make a partial payment today?"
  • "If you are facing financial difficulty, please speak to our team — we may be able to help."
  • "Your account will be reported to the credit bureau if the payment is not received by [date]."

✗ WHAT YOU MUST NEVER SAY

  • "I will send someone to your house" (unless a formal visit has been notified and approved)
  • "I will tell your employer about this"
  • "You will go to jail if you don't pay"
  • "Pay now or else..." (threats of any kind)
  • Any abusive or caste/religion-based language

Social Media — Complete Prohibition

You must never:

  • Post a borrower's name, photo, contact details, or loan information on any social media platform
  • Share borrower information in WhatsApp groups, Telegram channels, or any public forum
  • Comment on a borrower's social media posts to pressure payment
This is a criminal offence under the DPDP Act 2023 and may also attract liability under the IT Act, 2000. It is also a direct violation of RBI guidelines.

No Cash Collections

Respo is a fully digital lender. We do not accept cash. If a borrower offers cash payment:

  1. Do not accept it under any circumstances.
  2. Guide the borrower to pay through the Zype app or via bank transfer to the designated account.
  3. If the borrower insists on cash, escalate to your supervisor immediately.
Module 05
Handling Borrower Calls
Practical guidance on how to structure effective, compliant borrower conversations.

The 5-Step Call Framework

Step Action Description
1 Identify yourself and confirm borrower identity. State your name, Respo Financial Capital, and confirm you are speaking with the borrower. Mention the call is recorded.
2 State the purpose clearly and respectfully. Mention the overdue amount and the number of days past due. Keep it factual — no threats, no drama.
3 Listen actively. Ask the borrower to explain their situation. Be patient. Do not interrupt.
4 Offer a resolution. Based on what the borrower tells you, offer appropriate next steps. If you are not authorised to offer a specific arrangement, escalate — do not promise something you cannot deliver.
5 Confirm and close. Summarise what was agreed, give the borrower a clear next step, and thank them for speaking with you. Log the call immediately.

Real Call Scenarios

Scenario A — Borrower says "I will pay next week"
✗ Wrong approach
"You said that last time too. I am going to report this and take action against you."
✓ Right approach
"Thank you for letting me know. Could I confirm the exact date you plan to make the payment? I will note this on your account. Please also be aware that additional late charges will apply for each day the payment remains overdue. Is there anything I can do to help you make the payment sooner?"
Scenario B — Borrower says "I lost my job and cannot pay"
✗ Wrong approach
"That is not our problem. You took the loan and you need to pay."
✓ Right approach
"I am sorry to hear that. I understand this must be a difficult time. Let me note your situation on the account and connect you with our team who handles hardship cases. In the meantime, if you can make even a partial payment today, it will reduce the penalties accumulating on your account. Would that be possible?"
Scenario C — Borrower becomes abusive on the call
✗ Wrong approach
Responding with equal aggression or making threats.
✓ Right approach
"I understand you are upset. I want to help resolve this for you, but I need us to have a calm conversation. If now is not a good time, I can call you back later. Alternatively, you can reach our Grievance Redressal Officer at [GRO contact]. Shall I give you that number?"

Then log the call, note the borrower's behaviour, and inform your supervisor.

Scenario D — Someone other than the borrower answers
✗ Wrong approach
"Please tell [Borrower Name] that they owe ₹X and we will take action."
✓ Right approach
"I am looking to speak with [Borrower Name]. Could you please let them know that someone from Respo Financial Capital called and ask them to call back at [Respo number]? Thank you."

Never reveal loan details to anyone other than the borrower or guarantor.

After Every Call — What to Log

Immediately after every call, log the following in the collections system:

  • Date and time of call
  • Who answered (borrower / third party / no answer)
  • Summary of the conversation
  • Any commitment made by the borrower (payment date/amount)
  • Next action and date
Incomplete logs are a compliance risk. During an RBI inspection, all call records must be available. Missing or inaccurate logs can result in adverse inspection findings for Respo.
Module 06
Special Situations
How to handle situations that require extra care and specific procedures.

Borrower Claims Financial Hardship

When a borrower genuinely cannot pay due to job loss, medical emergency, or other hardship:

  1. Listen with empathy. Do not dismiss or trivialise their situation.
  2. Acknowledge the difficulty: "I understand this is a difficult time."
  3. Tell the borrower that Respo has a process for hardship cases and you will flag their account for review.
  4. Do NOT promise any specific restructuring, waiver, or arrangement — you are not authorised to do so. Escalate to your supervisor.
  5. Log the call with "Hardship — escalate for review" and notify your supervisor immediately.
Respo's policy: We assess financial hardship cases on their merits. A borrower in genuine distress who is communicating openly is treated differently from one who is avoiding contact. Your job is to identify and escalate hardship cases — not to resolve them unilaterally.

Borrower Disputes the Loan or Amount

If a borrower says they did not take this loan, do not recognise it, or disputes the outstanding amount:

  1. Do not argue. Take note of the dispute.
  2. Tell the borrower: "I understand you are disputing this. I will flag this on your account and our team will review it. In the meantime, please do not make any payment until the dispute is resolved."
  3. Log the dispute clearly and escalate to your supervisor immediately.
  4. Do not continue collections activity on a disputed account until the dispute is reviewed by the appropriate team.

Borrower Mentions Legal Action or Police Complaint

If a borrower says they will file a police complaint, approach a consumer forum, or contact RBI:

  • Do not panic or become defensive.
  • Respond calmly: "You are absolutely entitled to approach any authority. Our Grievance Redressal Officer can also help resolve your concern — let me give you that contact."
  • Log the call and inform your supervisor.
  • Do not make any threats in response.

Borrower Requests a No-Contact Period

If a borrower explicitly requests that you stop contacting them:

  1. Note the request on the account.
  2. Inform the borrower that the account remains active and dues continue to accrue.
  3. Escalate to your supervisor — do not unilaterally stop all collections activity without authorisation.
  4. Your supervisor will decide the appropriate next step (legal notice, agency handoff etc.).

Deceased Borrower

This is a sensitive situation that requires care:

  • Do not continue collections calls to the deceased borrower's number once informed of the death.
  • Speak to the supervisor immediately and escalate to the legal/recovery team.
  • Communications with the legal heir (if any) must follow a specific process — do not improvise.
  • Exercise empathy and professionalism at all times when interacting with the family.
Module 07
Grievances and Escalations
Understanding how to handle borrower complaints and when to escalate.

Every Borrower Has the Right to Complain

Under Respo's Fair Practice Code and RBI guidelines, every borrower has the right to raise a grievance. You must never discourage, dismiss, or obstruct a borrower from raising a complaint.

Respo's Grievance Redressal Structure:
Level 1 — Customer Service Team: First point of contact. Must respond within 5 working days.
Level 2 — Grievance Redressal Officer (GRO): Escalation if Level 1 is unsatisfactory. Must respond within 15 working days.
Level 3 — RBI Ombudsman: If the grievance is unresolved within 30 days or the borrower is unsatisfied with Respo's resolution.

When a Borrower Raises a Complaint During a Collections Call

  1. Listen fully to the complaint without interrupting.
  2. Acknowledge: "Thank you for bringing this to my attention. I take this seriously."
  3. Provide the GRO contact details: email and phone number available on the Respo website and the Zype app.
  4. Log the complaint on the collections system with full details.
  5. Inform your supervisor immediately.
  6. Pause collections activity on the account pending complaint resolution — check with your supervisor on this.

Complaints About You Specifically

If a borrower complains about your behaviour — how you spoke to them, the time you called, something you said — take it seriously:

  • Do not be defensive or dismissive.
  • Do not contact the borrower again on the same complaint without supervisor authorisation.
  • Report the complaint to your supervisor immediately.
  • All calls are recorded — the recording will be reviewed as part of the grievance process.
Consequence of a validated complaint against you: If a borrower's complaint about your conduct is found to be valid, it will result in disciplinary action under Respo's HR policy — which may include a formal warning, suspension, or termination depending on the severity.

Escalation to RBI Ombudsman

If a borrower tells you they are going to approach the RBI Ombudsman:

  • Acknowledge their right to do so.
  • Provide the RBI CMS portal link: cms.rbi.org.in
  • Inform your supervisor immediately so the complaint can be tracked and responded to within the 30-day RBI timeline.
Module 08
Data and Privacy Rules
Your obligations under the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and Respo's data policy.

What Is Borrower Data?

Every piece of information about a borrower that you have access to — their name, phone number, address, employer, loan amount, repayment history, bank account — is personal data protected by law.

You may only use borrower data for one purpose: to carry out your collections responsibilities on behalf of Respo. Using it for any other purpose is a violation of the DPDP Act 2023 and Respo's data policy.

What You Must Never Do With Borrower Data

  • Share a borrower's name, address, loan details, or any other information with anyone other than the borrower or guarantor
  • Save borrower data on your personal phone or personal email
  • Screenshot or photograph collections system data on your personal device
  • Discuss borrower information with colleagues unnecessarily — access only what you need for your work
  • Share borrower data on social media, WhatsApp groups, or any external platform

If You Suspect a Data Breach

If you notice that borrower data may have been accessed, shared, or compromised — even accidentally:

  1. Do not try to fix it yourself or cover it up.
  2. Report it to your supervisor and the Compliance team immediately.
  3. Under the DPDP Act 2023, Respo must report data breaches to the Data Protection Board — so timing matters.

Borrower Rights Under DPDP Act 2023

Every borrower has the right to:

Right What It Means for You
Access their data If a borrower asks what data we hold, direct them to the Zype app or the GRO — do not answer data access queries yourself
Correct inaccurate data If a borrower says their details are wrong, note it and escalate — do not attempt to correct data yourself
Raise a data complaint Direct them to the GRO contact on the Zype app

Using the Collections System Responsibly

  • Log out of the collections system when you are not actively using it
  • Never share your login credentials with anyone
  • Do not access accounts that are not assigned to you
  • Report any system errors or unusual access you notice to your supervisor
